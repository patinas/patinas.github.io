Installing Better xCloud on my Android TV

24 October 2026 · 3 min read · Android, Xbox, home lab

The job

The Mi Box S from my last post had another task tonight: install Better xCloud, the Android app for Xbox Cloud Gaming, and get it signed in. Xbox Cloud Gaming streams games from Microsoft's servers to xbox.com/play, so an app on the TV box is all the hardware the setup needs.

What Better xCloud is

Better xCloud is a free, open-source app from the developer redphx, built around the xbox.com/play website. The developer lists support for 1080p streaming, a sharper picture and more control over the stream as features. Those are the app's claimed capabilities, not something I have measured on this box. It needs Android 8 or later, so my Android 9 box is inside the requirement.

What your account unlocks

The app costs nothing. What you can play depends on your Microsoft account. A free Microsoft account with an Xbox profile already covers the free-to-play games at xbox.com/play, like Fortnite. The Game Pass catalog needs a Game Pass plan that includes cloud gaming. Either way you need a decent internet connection. On a TV, plan on pairing a controller over Bluetooth; touch controls, where games support them, are for phones and tablets.

Installing it

The developer distributes the app only through the project's GitHub releases page, github.com/redphx/better-xcloud-android/releases, and warns against copies from anywhere else. Treat that page as the official distribution source. The current release, 0.24.2, ships a single APK of about 6 MB, so there is no architecture choice to make.

On an Android TV the steps are: get the APK onto the box (a USB drive works, and so does a downloader app from the Play Store), open it from a file manager, and install. Android asks you to allow installs from unknown sources for the app doing the installing. Grant it for that one app, install, then open Better xCloud from the app row.

On my box, version 0.24.2 installed cleanly and opened without problems. That is as far as this article's claim goes: installed and opened, on this specific box, tonight.

Sign-in, and what is not proven yet

Sign-in happens on Microsoft's login pages inside the app. The developer's documentation states that the app never sends your Xbox account or token anywhere else. That is the developer's statement, not something I have independently verified. The sign-in on my box was started tonight. What I have not done yet is play a full game stream on it. The developer is direct about budget Android TV hardware: expect problems on cheap devices, and the Mi Box S is a budget box. The real test, an actual game running smoothly, is still ahead.

Safety advice if you try this

  • Get the APK from the project's official GitHub releases page. The developer warns against copies from other sites.
  • Give the unknown-sources permission only to the app doing the installing, and turn it off again afterwards.
  • Sign in only on Microsoft's login pages.

Where it stands

Next step is the streaming test: pair a controller, start a game and see whether this budget box can carry a stream.
