Turning my wardrobe into an AI outfit planner

30 October 2026 · 4 min read · AI, wardrobe, automation

The idea

On 29 October I asked my AI assistant for a clothing inventory: I send photos of all my clothes plus a full-body photo, and it puts together outfit combinations. On top of that I asked for a virtual try-on: a generated image of me wearing the suggested outfit, which the assistant then checks with vision.

Building the inventory

The work on my side was simple. I laid a piece of clothing flat or hung it up, photographed it and sent the photo on WhatsApp. The assistant gave each item a number and added it to a private spreadsheet with photo, name, colour and notes. The first items were also cleaned onto a plain white background for a uniform catalogue look. After two rounds of photographing, the inventory holds 67 numbered items, and the original photos are saved privately.

Some items arrived twice from different angles. Repeat photos were grouped instead of counted twice, and uncertain photos were saved separately for me to confirm rather than guessed at. Items can also be flagged: a pair of jeans missing a button was marked for repair and kept out of suggestions; once the button was back on, it was marked ready again.

The rules it dresses me by

A suggestion is not random. Every outfit follows rules I set:

  • Check the weather in Aalborg and say if the layers are too much or too little.
  • Never suggest an item marked for repair.
  • Braces instead of a belt for trousers without a drawstring.
  • Always include an AI try-on image.

The try-on image

Every suggestion comes with a generated image of me wearing the outfit on a removed background. The first attempt was a collage of pasted-in clothing photos, and it looked wrong, so the assistant rejected its own result and redid it as a proper AI try-on. A vision check then compares the image against the original photos for changed details and warped proportions.

One honest limit: the preview shows roughly how an outfit looks, not how it fits. It says nothing reliable about size or how fabric drapes. The generated images are archived to my Google Photos so I can scroll back through earlier suggestions.

What I got out of it

Spread over two days of photographing, the inventory grew to 67 numbered items with private photos, rules for weather, repairs and braces, and an AI try-on image with each suggestion. The next step is simply to use it: ask for an outfit and get a suggestion with a preview image.
